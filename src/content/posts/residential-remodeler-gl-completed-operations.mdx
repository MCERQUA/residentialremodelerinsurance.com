A kitchen remodel looks perfect on punch-out day. Three months later, the homeowner notices water pooling beneath the new cabinets — a plumbing connection that worked fine at first shifted under load. The damage is $18,000 and climbing. Who pays?

If you're a residential remodeler without completed-operations coverage, the answer is probably you.

## What Completed-Operations Coverage Is

General liability (GL) insurance has two distinct exposure buckets:

- **Premises/operations:** Covers bodily injury and property damage that happen *while you're actively working* on the jobsite.
- **Completed operations:** Covers bodily injury and property damage that arise *after you've finished* and left the property.

Residential remodeling work — kitchens, bathrooms, additions, basement conversions, whole-home renovations — carries substantial completed-operations exposure. Defects in framing, tile work, plumbing rough-ins, or electrical that aren't immediately apparent can cause damage for months or years after project completion.

Most standard GL policies include a completed-operations section, but the coverage limits may be lower than your premises/operations limits, or the policy may not be structured for the residential contractor market. It's worth asking exactly how your policy handles this.

## The "Occurrence" vs. "Claims-Made" Distinction

For remodelers, occurrence-based GL is generally preferable to claims-made. Here's why:

An **occurrence policy** covers you for any damage *caused during* the policy period, regardless of when the claim is filed. If a tile installation causes moisture damage that isn't discovered until two years later, your policy that was active during the installation responds — even if you've since switched carriers.

A **claims-made policy** only covers claims *filed while the policy is in force*. If you cancel or switch carriers without purchasing extended-reporting (tail) coverage, claims filed after cancellation aren't covered even for work done while the policy was active.

For long-tail exposures like residential remodeling, occurrence coverage gives you much cleaner protection.

## Common Completed-Operations Claims in Remodeling

The claims we see most often at Contractors Choice Agency involve:

**Water intrusion after bathroom tile work.** Improper waterproofing behind tile leads to moisture infiltration that damages walls, subfloor, and eventually ceiling joists below. These claims often surface 6–18 months post-completion.

**Structural issues from additions and expansions.** Header sizing, load path, and connection details that passed inspection can still fail under seasonal movement or atypical loads. Structural claims can be large.

**Electrical fire damage.** Wiring defects may not immediately cause problems but can contribute to fires long after the crew leaves. Fire claims frequently exceed $50,000.

**HVAC and insulation defects.** Improper attic insulation or HVAC installation affecting energy performance or causing condensation issues is increasingly common as energy codes become more demanding.

**Flooring failures.** Hardwood or LVP installations that weren't properly acclimated or installed with adequate expansion gaps can buckle or cup months after installation.

## How Much Completed-Operations Coverage Do You Need?

For most residential remodelers, a $1 million per-occurrence / $2 million aggregate GL policy is the minimum acceptable baseline. If you're doing larger projects — full home renovations, additions over 1,000 square feet, or custom kitchen work — $2 million per-occurrence with a $4 million aggregate is worth considering.

Some GCs and project owners will require specific completed-operations sublimits in your policy. Review your subcontract requirements before you start a job.

A commercial umbrella can efficiently layer additional limits above your GL and is often the most cost-effective way to increase your completed-operations protection.
